# Blue Clay Clean

## Fixed Stage Contract

Every page is a fixed `750×1320` stage. Presentation shells scale the stage uniformly without rearranging columns, wrapping modules differently, or cropping critical content. Maintain 54 px horizontal safe margins. Photographic gradients and texture smears may bleed; claims, ingredient leaders, comparison labels, and method notes stay within y=58–1266.

The system balances two propositions that often conflict visually: deep cleansing and retained comfort. Dark navy and mineral texture establish removal power; mist blue, white space, fluid spheres, and measured editorial copy establish hydration and calm.

## Overview

The audited source contains eight long sections: a mineral-blue hero, an oil/dry-skin problem frame, a two-in-one benefit overview, a deep-cleansing mechanism, a moisturizing system with floating ingredients, a blue-clay texture and foam story, a removal comparison, and a three-step use guide.

The reusable narrative is compact and works well as a 6–8 page internal deck:

1. Hero: one balanced cleansing promise.
2. Tension: remove excess without framing skin as an enemy.
3. Overview: map four benefits into cleansing and comfort roles.
4. Mechanism: explain capture, lift, rinse, and retained moisture.
5. Hydration/ingredients: show roles around a central product or skin field.
6. Texture: clay, spread, foam, and rinse behavior.
7. Evidence: removal result with controlled comparison conditions.
8. Use: amount, water condition, motion, and rinse.

For a shorter story, combine steps 2 and 3, and combine steps 5 and 6. Never merge mechanism and removal evidence if that would make the diagram appear to prove the result.

## Visual DNA

### Mineral descent

The hero descends from deep navy into pale icy blue, like pigment dispersing into water. A pale blue tube or object stands against the transition. One clay or powder smear introduces tactility. White type sits in the dark calm region.

### Editorial clarity

Most explanatory pages are white. Black headlines may contain one mineral-blue phrase. Images are large, decisively cropped, and integrated with copy. A blue benefit band may anchor the bottom third, but it should carry genuine structure rather than decoration.

### Ingredient orbit

Hydration or formula pages use a few translucent amber, blue, or clear spheres with thin leader lines. The spheres represent defined roles or ingredient groups. Their glassy beauty supports hierarchy; it is never a substitute for a real formula explanation.

## Color System

### Roles

- `deep_navy #071B2A` establishes depth, mineral strength, and hero contrast.
- `slate_blue #355B73` supports dark transitions and diagram layers.
- `mineral #5985A2` is the main accent for benefits, headings, and mechanism paths.
- `mist_blue #ACC2D1` builds gentle bands and product harmony.
- `ice #E7EFF2` is a light diagram or hydration field.
- `white #FFFFFF` is the primary editorial canvas.
- `ink #111719` is the main text color.
- `copy #626B6F` supports explanation and sources.
- `line #CDD8DD` structures comparisons.
- `amber_specimen #B98B56` appears only in ingredient or oil-related specimens.

### Ratios

Use one dark hero or chapter page for every three to five white pages. On white pages, mineral blue occupies 8–18% of the stage; on a benefit-band page it may reach 30%. Amber should remain under 3% and always correspond to an actual specimen or defined formula role.

### Contrast

Use white on deep navy, ink on white/ice, and ink or deep navy on mist blue. Use darker slate_blue rather than mineral for small text. Do not set long white body copy on mid-blue. Ingredient leaders need both color and text labels so meaning survives grayscale.

## Typography

The source face is not bundled. Use the approved CJK system stack with dense display weight and clear editorial body type.

### Scale

- Hero claim: 62–76 px, bold, two to three lines.
- Section headline: 42–54 px.
- Benefit title: 26–34 px.
- Proof number: 62–90 px.
- Body: 20–24 px, 1.5–1.65 line height.
- Mechanism label: 17–21 px.
- Caption: 16–19 px.
- Method/source: 14–16 px.

### Blue phrase pattern

On white pages, one phrase in the section headline may use mineral or slate blue, followed by black completion copy. The color phrase names the functional axis—cleansing, moisture, or texture. Do not color every noun. On dark pages, keep the headline white and use blue through material, not text.

### CJK breaking

Break by tension and resolution: remove excess / retain comfort, cleanse / do not strip, texture / rinse behavior. Keep “不” with the following verb or adjective. Attach percentages and duration units. Avoid leaving a single Chinese character on the last line.

## Layout System

### Grid

Use 6 columns between x=54 and x=696 with 14–18 px gutters. Heroes center or slightly offset the product. Editorial pages use a 3/3 portrait-and-copy split, a 2/4 mechanism split, or a full-width lower benefit band.

### Vertical zones

- Eyebrow/identity: y=58–112
- Claim: y=132–314
- Main image or system: y=330–1010
- Method/source: y=1080–1266

Benefit bands usually begin between y=850 and y=960 and can run to the bottom edge, provided all labels remain above the 54 px bottom safe margin.

### Density

A typical page supports one image, one headline, two short paragraphs, and up to four labels. Ingredient orbit pages may hold four role labels. Mechanism pages use no more than four process states. If a page needs both a process diagram and a test comparison, split it.

## Component Specifications

### MineralHero

Use a vertical or diagonal deep-navy-to-ice gradient. Put the white hero claim in the darker upper field. A pale blue product object occupies 40–55% of stage height. Add one clay, powder, or pigment specimen near the base. Keep the mark count low; no benefit icon row on the hero.

### SkinTensionFrame

Describe the tension between oil, residue, dryness, or tightness without stigmatizing the person. Use one portrait or macro skin context plus two opposing short statements. The resolution line appears in mineral blue. If a clinical skin image is unavailable, use a neutral surface or diagram.

### TwoInOneOverview

Show two principal systems—cleansing and comfort—feeding four outcomes. Use a central portrait or product with a 2×2 `BenefitBand` below. Every outcome receives a type label: removal, feel, moisture, or texture. Avoid claiming one mechanism explains them all.

### BenefitBand

A full-width mineral or mist-blue field divided by thin lines. Use four cells maximum. On a dark mineral band, titles are white and qualifiers are pale ice; on mist blue, titles are ink. Icons are optional and must be from one monoline family.

### CleansingMechanism

Use a four-step sequence such as contact / capture / lift / rinse. Show skin or surface layers accurately and consistently. Mineral blue marks the cleansing path; amber may identify oil or residue. Use arrows sparingly and label conceptual imagery as schematic. The final state should show clean surface without depicting impossible pore closure.

### HydrationOrbit

Use a central skin, product, or water field with 3–4 orbiting `IngredientSphere` objects. Each leader terminates at a short role label and one supporting sentence. Arrange leaders to avoid crossings. Ingredient names may appear, but role and evidence status are primary.

### IngredientSphere

A clear, pale blue, or amber translucent sphere with one highlight and soft contact shadow. Keep sphere sizes varied but purposeful. Do not render a galaxy of decorative bubbles. A sphere may represent one ingredient, complex, or measured role—not all three.

### ClaySpecimen

Show a large blue-clay smear, ribbon, or dollop on white or ice. Annotate color, spread, cushion, or residue with no more than three labels. Include the package edge or a caption so the specimen is not mistaken for food, paint, or unrelated material.

### FoamSequence

Use three matched macro states: product, emulsified foam, rinse. Keep lighting and scale stable. State whether the foam is dense, fine, quick, or low in volume only if observed or specified. Decorative bubbles do not demonstrate cleansing performance.

### RemovalEvidence

Use paired swatches or matched surface images before and after a controlled wash step. State product applied, amount or method, water condition, and number of passes where known. A large result may summarize the observation; it cannot replace the conditions. If the comparison involves sunscreen or color product, name it generically unless rights allow otherwise.

### ThreeStepUse

Use amount / emulsify / cleanse-and-rinse, or another honest three-step sequence. Each frame includes one verb, a clear hand/face/product crop, and the condition that matters. Keep water temperature and avoidance instructions near the corresponding action.

### MethodNote and PageMarker

Use 14–16 px gray text inside the safe area. Evidence pages always include a method note; conceptual pages include a schematic note. Page markers are optional and can use a small blue rule plus chapter name.

## Page Blueprint Library

Use these blueprints to preserve the source board’s content richness without squeezing several jobs onto one stage.

### B01 — Mineral Promise

- Keep one white action-and-comfort claim in the dark region.
- Use one pale product object and one blue mineral specimen.
- Let the gradient explain depth; do not add feature cards.
- Add a qualifier only when the promise contains measured language.
- Preserve a quiet lower or side region around the object silhouette.

### B02 — Skin or Surface Tension

- Describe the baseline in neutral, respectful terms.
- Pair excess or residue with the risk of over-removal.
- Use one human or surface context, not an alarmist collage.
- Resolve the tension with one blue sentence.
- Avoid ingredient or result claims at this stage.

### B03 — Two-System Overview

- Name cleansing and comfort as separate systems.
- Route both systems into four labeled outcomes.
- Use one central portrait or product anchor.
- Put the four outcomes in a lower 2×2 benefit band.
- Identify which statements are experience, mechanism, or evidence.

### B04 — Contact to Rinse

- Show baseline, contact, capture/lift, and rinse.
- Keep layer geometry consistent between states.
- Use mineral blue for intervention and amber only for defined residue.
- Label the page as schematic.
- End with an outcome sentence proportional to the mechanism.

### B05 — Hydration Roles

- Place the product, skin, or water field at the center.
- Use three or four spheres with distinct, defined roles.
- Route leaders outward without crossing.
- Separate ingredient presence from supported outcome.
- Provide a source note for quantified moisture statements.

### B06 — Texture and Foam

- Show clay specimen, emulsified state, and rinse state at matched scale.
- Annotate no more than three observable properties.
- Keep package context visible on at least one state.
- Avoid decorative bubbles that imply performance.
- Put user condition or water amount in the caption when relevant.

### B07 — Removal Ledger

- Identify the applied material or comparator.
- Match surface, amount, exposure, camera, and lighting.
- Show before, defined wash step, and after.
- Put conditions and number of passes beside the result.
- State whether the conclusion is observed, measured, or illustrative.

### B08 — Use and Caution

- Use amount, emulsify, cleanse, and rinse as the basic logic.
- Keep hand, face, or product images consistent.
- Attach water condition and timing to their exact step.
- Put avoidance and frequency guidance in a visible footer.
- Close with one operational reminder.

## Copy Architecture

### Balanced claim

Use `[remove or control] + [retain or support]`. The grammar must visibly hold both sides; avoid placing all emphasis on removal and relegating comfort to fine print.

### Mechanism sentence

Use `[material/process] contacts [target], supports [action], and is removed by [rinse condition]`. If the pathway is conceptual, use cautious language and a schematic label.

### Ingredient-role sentence

Use `[ingredient or complex] is included for [defined formulation role]`. Follow with the actual evidence status. Do not jump from presence to guaranteed user outcome.

### Removal result

Use `[observation] after [defined wash method] on [defined material]`. Attach amount, water, passes, comparator, and source. Keep the large display line simpler than the full method, but never detached from it.

### Use instruction

Begin with a verb and specify quantity, water, duration, or avoidance only when operationally relevant. A visible completion cue is more useful than a vague “massage thoroughly.”

## Content and Evidence Discipline

The story separates five meanings:

1. Cleansing mechanism: how contact and removal are intended to work.
2. Cleansing result: what was observed under stated conditions.
3. Moisture or comfort mechanism: formulation role or proposed support.
4. Sensory experience: texture, foam, rinse, and after-feel.
5. Usage: amount, sequence, frequency, and cautions.

Do not let a sphere illustration prove hydration, a foam macro prove removal, or a blue color imply mineral efficacy. Whenever a result is numeric, retain its unit, comparator, time point, method, and source. When the content is qualitative, name it as an observed or reported experience.

## Image Direction

Preferred assets: pale blue product cutouts, dark navy-to-ice gradients, powder or clay smears, neutral portraits holding a product, clean skin/surface diagrams, translucent amber and blue spheres, consistent foam macros, and matched removal swatches.

Light should be cool and soft, with crisp product edges. Skin is represented respectfully and naturally. Do not over-retouch texture or use alarmist close-ups. Make all comparison photographs consistent in camera position and exposure.

## Depth and Effects

Use one soft product shadow on the hero and restrained contact shadows beneath spheres. White editorial pages are otherwise flat. Gradient depth belongs to the hero and occasional mechanism backdrop, not every card. Use transparency and overlap sparingly so leaders and labels remain clear.

Avoid cyan neon, heavy bevels, underwater caustic overlays, excessive bubble particles, glassmorphism cards, and multiple shadow levels. The system is mineral and clean, not aquatic fantasy.

## Shapes and Iconography

Shapes derive from tubes, clay ribbons, spheres, foam cells, thin leader lines, and broad rectangular benefit bands. Corners are mostly square or subtly rounded, 0–14 px. Icons are monoline at 1.5–2 px. Avoid droplet icons for every benefit; use text hierarchy when the meaning is already clear.

## Don't

1. Don't turn the system into a generic medical-blue interface.
2. Don't fill every page with a navy-to-blue gradient.
3. Don't use foam, bubbles, or blue pigment as proof of cleansing efficacy.
4. Don't stigmatize oily, dry, or textured skin through alarmist imagery.
5. Don't imply that one mechanism proves every cleansing and comfort outcome.
6. Don't draw impossible pore opening, closure, or extraction behavior.
7. Don't use decorative ingredient bubbles without labels and roles.
8. Don't let orbit leaders cross or terminate ambiguously.
9. Don't compare unmatched before/after surfaces, lighting, or product amounts.
10. Don't omit water condition, passes, amount, or comparator from removal evidence.
11. Don't use more than four cells in a benefit band.
12. Don't split Chinese numbers from units or negation from its verb.
13. Don't reflow the internal stage at different viewport sizes.
14. Don't bundle the source font, product photography, brand marks, or claims.

## Responsive, Presenter, Animation, and Print

The responsive shell uniformly scales and centers the complete `750×1320` stage. On small screens, complete pages scroll vertically. Presenter controls and notes remain outside the stage.

Motion can reveal the mineral gradient, sequence contact/capture/lift/rinse, settle 3–4 ingredient spheres into place, or crossfade matched removal evidence. Use 350–700 ms transitions and stop all movement before detailed reading. Do not loop bubbles or liquid behind text. Reduced-motion mode shows stable final states.

For print, test separation between ice and white, dark detail in navy, and leader-line visibility. Keep diagram rules at least 1.5 px and source notes at least 14 px. Confirm transparent spheres do not disappear when flattened.

## CJK Guidance

Use direct language that distinguishes removal, moisture, feel, and use. Break headings at semantic tension or resolution. Keep “不” attached to the next word and numbers attached to units. Avoid stacking vague four-character benefit phrases. Proprietary source typography is reference-only; use the approved system CJK stack.

## Iteration Guide

Build the eight-page skeleton first and label every page as promise, tension, overview, mechanism, formula, texture, evidence, or use. Check that none is trying to perform three jobs. Then review color rhythm: one dark hero, primarily white editorial pages, and targeted blue fields.

Run a proof audit on every sphere, arrow, foam macro, and comparison. Ask what it represents and whether the caption states that honestly. Finally, view the deck in grayscale and at export size; labels and process order must remain understandable without color or animation.

## Known Gaps

The audit was based on a composite Figma board rather than original research files, formula documents, test reports, or licensed image packages. Hidden nodes, image-internal text, exact blend settings, retouching, comparison conditions, ingredient substantiation, and usage safety were not independently verified. The 10725 px board must be split into fixed slides. All source products, claims, portraits, ingredient descriptions, test results, and branded comparison items require replacement or approval before reuse.
